A language learning app targeting 2-year-olds (core user; ages 2–5 is aspirational but unresolved). Each session shows two photos and plays recorded voice naming one; the child taps the match. Sessions have time limits and track per-word accuracy to randomize future words. The owner assumes children prefer a parent's voice (validated against some articles) but has no evidence parents sustain recording—fallback audio is available. Parents can view accuracy trends in a private menu if they wish. The app is audio-first by design, currently scoped for hearing children. Toddlers complete sessions alone without reading. First customers: peers aged ~35, validated through reviews, feedback, and focus groups.

## Must
- Parents can choose the target language from five options: English, German, Turkish, Spanish, French
- The app tracks per-word accuracy over days (attempts, accuracy, trend per word) and shows it to parents
- Each session has a time limit
- A toddler can complete a session alone, without reading and without parent help mid-session
- The child sees neutral in-session progress (e.g., filled dots) with no rewards attached

## Never
- No gamification, no rewards — no badges, streaks, points, stickers or celebration screens; neutral progress dots are not a reward
- No mode that replaces audio as the primary channel — audio-first design is fundamental
